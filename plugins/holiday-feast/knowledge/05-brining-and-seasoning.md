# Brining and Seasoning

## Why salt ahead of time

Salt dissolves some of the muscle proteins, so the meat holds on to more of its own juice when it cooks. That's why salted-ahead turkey tastes juicier and is seasoned all the way through, not just on the skin. Herbs, citrus and spices in a brine mostly add aroma to the surface; **salt does the work.**

Pick **one** method: dry brine **or** wet brine **or** a pre-salted (kosher/enhanced) bird. Combining them makes the turkey too salty.

## Dry brine (recommended)

**What:** Rub salt directly on the turkey and refrigerate it uncovered.

**Why it's the default here:** no bucket or bag, takes almost no fridge space beyond the bird, and the skin dries out so it crisps in the oven.

**How:**

1. Thaw the turkey completely. Remove neck and giblets. Pat dry.
2. Use **1 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt per 4–5 lb of turkey** (12–14 lb bird: about 3–4 tbsp). For Morton kosher salt use about half that volume; it's much denser. Table salt is even denser and harder to spread evenly; avoid it.
3. Optional: mix in 1 tsp per 4–5 lb black pepper, dried herbs, or an **unsalted** rub. If you add a salted rub, reduce the salt.
4. Optional for crisper skin: 1 tbsp baking powder (not soda) for the whole bird.
5. Gently loosen the breast skin with your fingers. Rub about a third of the salt under the skin on the breast, a third over the skin, and the rest on the thighs, back and inside the cavity.
6. Set on a rack over a tray in the fridge. Leave **uncovered for at least the last 12–24 hours** (loosely cover earlier if the fridge is crowded).
7. Time: **1–3 days.** Even 12 hours helps. Don't rinse before roasting.

## Wet brine

**What:** Submerge the turkey in salted water.

**Pros:** very forgiving, slightly juicier-tasting result to some people.
**Cons:** needs a large food-safe container or brining bag and must stay below 40°F for the whole time (a fridge, or a cooler with ice monitored with a thermometer); skin is softer and paler unless you dry the bird afterward.

**How:**

1. Dissolve **about 1 cup Diamond Crystal kosher salt per gallon of water** (≈ ¾ cup Morton kosher, ≈ ½ cup table salt). Heat part of the water to dissolve salt and aromatics, then add ice or cold water until the brine is fully cold, below 40°F.
2. Use enough brine to cover the bird (a 14 lb turkey usually needs 1½–2 gallons).
3. Brine **12–24 hours**. Longer gets salty and spongy.
4. Remove, pat very dry, and ideally refrigerate uncovered for several hours or overnight so the skin dries.
5. Discard the brine. Don't reuse it or make gravy from it.

**Using a commercial brine kit** (such as Madison Park's Chairman of the Bird EASYBRINE): follow the kit's own salt and time instructions, since its salt level differs from homemade. See [16-madison-park-foods.md](16-madison-park-foods.md).

## Do NOT brine if

- The label says **kosher, self-basting, enhanced, pre-brined, or "contains up to X% solution."**
- You're short on safe cold space for a wet brine.
- Anyone at the table is on a strict low-sodium diet (consider an unsalted rub and serving gravy on the side).

## Rubs and seasonings

- **Unsalted rubs** (herbs, citrus peel, spices) can go on any time: during the dry brine, in the herb butter, or just before roasting.
- **Salted rubs** count toward your salt. If you dry brine, use them lightly or only as a finishing seasoning.
- To help a rub stick: pat the skin dry, then rub with softened butter or oil before adding the rub. Dry herbs on dry skin fall off.
- **Sugar-containing rubs** (BBQ-style) brown and can burn at high heat. Watch the skin and tent with foil if it darkens too fast.

## Herb butter

A compound butter under and over the skin adds flavor and helps browning.

- 4–8 tbsp softened unsalted butter (more for big birds)
- 1–2 tbsp chopped fresh herbs (sage, thyme, rosemary, parsley) or 1–2 tsp dried herbs or an unsalted rub
- Optional: zest of 1 lemon or orange, 2 cloves grated garlic

Mash together. After dry brining, spread some under the breast skin and the rest over the skin just before roasting. If the turkey was wet-brined or is kosher, use unsalted butter and no extra salt.

**Madison Park suggestion:** the company suggests adding its Chairman of the Bird rub to seasoned butter or applying it all over just before roasting. The rub is sold as salt-free per reseller listings, which makes it a natural partner for a salt dry brine.

## Aromatics in the cavity

Quartered onion, halved lemon or orange, a halved head of garlic, and herb sprigs. These lightly perfume the meat and drippings. Fill loosely. Don't pack the cavity: air needs to circulate.
