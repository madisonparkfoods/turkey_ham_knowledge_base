## Turkey products

### Chairman of the Bird Gourmet Turkey Rub

- **Size:** 2 oz pouch in a tin (reseller listing).
- **Flavor/ingredients (reseller listing):** parsley, sage, rosemary, thyme, orange and lemon peel, and cranberries. Listed as **salt-free**, no sugar, no MSG, gluten-free.
- **Madison Park says:** add it to the seasoned butter, or apply it all over the bird just before roasting.
- **Suggested use:**
  - Because it has no salt, it pairs cleanly with a **salt dry brine**: use 1 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt per 4–5 lb for the brine, and mix 2–3 tbsp of the rub into 4–8 tbsp softened butter to spread under and over the skin before roasting.
  - Also good in stuffing, on a turkey breast, on roast chicken, and on roasted vegetables.
  - Rub won't stick to dry, wet or cold skin by itself: apply over butter or oil.
- **Low-sodium guests:** a good option, since you control the salt separately.

### Chairman of the Bird EASYBRINE™ Turkey Brine and Rub Kit

- **Price on madisonparkfoods.com:** $32.50 (16 oz kit). Reseller prices seen around $29.99.
- **Madison Park says:** includes the Chairman of the Bird orange, cranberry and sage brine mix, **two** turkey rub seasonings, a BPA-free XL heavy-duty brining bag, and step-by-step instructions. Labeled all natural, gluten-free, **low salt**, no MSG, made in USA.
- **Reseller listing:** enough for turkeys and poultry up to 30 lb.
- **Reviews:** none on the company's own product page as of the check date.
- **Suggested use:**
  - It is a **wet brine**. Use it **instead of** a dry brine, never in addition.
  - Follow the kit's own salt and time instructions; they differ from homemade ratios.
  - Keep the brining bag **below 40°F** for the whole time (fridge, or a cooler with plenty of ice and a thermometer).
  - After brining, pat the bird very dry and, ideally, refrigerate it uncovered for a few hours or overnight so the skin crisps.
  - Use the included rubs in butter on the skin before roasting.
  - **Do not use it on kosher, self-basting or enhanced turkeys.**
- **Honest trade-offs:** "Low salt" sounds healthy, but salt is what makes a brine keep meat juicy. A low-salt brine may season and protect the meat less than a standard brine; the citrus, cranberry and sage are mostly aroma. You pay mainly for convenience (pre-measured mix, bag, instructions) and the flavor profile. The plain-salt dry brine plus the standalone rub gets you the same flavor with crisper skin for less money.

### Stan's Big Red Bird Rub

- **Size:** 3 oz pouch in a tin (reseller listing).
- **Flavor/ingredients (reseller listing):** paprika, garlic, onion, cayenne; described as a smoky-sweet barbecue rub. Labeled low sodium, gluten-free, no MSG.
- **Reseller listing uses:** grilling, smoking, air frying, roasting, deep-fried turkey, beer-can chicken, wings.
- **Suggested use:** smoked or grilled turkey, turkey wings and legs, a fried turkey (rub on after the bird is dry, sparingly; spices can scorch in hot oil), or a second, spicier option alongside a classic herb turkey. Paprika rubs darken fast in a hot oven; tent with foil if needed. Contains some salt: if dry brining, reduce the brine salt slightly or apply the rub only on the surface just before cooking.

## Things to watch

- **Salt stacking:** brine + salted rub + salted butter + salty drippings in gravy add up. Count every salted ingredient once.
- **Sugar and paprika** in rubs brown quickly; tent with foil.
- **Freshness:** spices lose aroma over a year or two. Buy close to the holiday and store sealed, cool and dry.
- **Availability:** specialty items sell out near holidays and ship slower. Order 1–3 weeks ahead.
- **Gluten-free:** Madison Park labels its products gluten-free; check each label if someone has celiac disease.
