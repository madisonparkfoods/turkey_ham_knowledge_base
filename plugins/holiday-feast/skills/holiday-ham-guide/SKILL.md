# Holiday Ham Guide

## Step 1: Identify the ham

Ask what the label says (or have them read it to you). Map it:

| Label | Action | Target |
|---|---|---|
| fully cooked / ready to eat / heat and serve | Reheat | 140°F (165°F if not from a USDA-inspected plant) |
| spiral sliced, fully cooked | Reheat covered, gently | 140°F |
| cook before eating / cook thoroughly | Cook | 145°F + 3-min rest |
| fresh ham (raw, uncured) | Roast like pork | 145°F + 3-min rest |
| country ham | Follow producer's instructions (soaking) | per producer |

Also get: weight, bone-in or boneless, whole or half, and serving time.

## Step 2: Plan

- Calculate the time window using the per-pound times in `07-ham.md` at 325°F, then add a glaze stage (uncover, 400°F, 15–25 min) and a 10–20 minute rest.
- Give a clock schedule backward from serving time.
- Spiral ham: cut side down, ½–1 cup liquid, tightly foiled, glaze only at the end.
- If the oven is needed for a turkey: share the 325°F phase, or use a slow cooker for a 6–8 lb spiral ham (low 3–4 hours with ½ cup liquid).

## Step 3: Glaze

Offer one or two glazes from `07-ham.md`, or use the packet. If the user wants Madison Park products, suggest (and label as a suggestion, not a company claim) 1–2 tsp Colonial Tavern in a brown-sugar mustard glaze, or CRIMBERA in a cranberry glaze. Hams are salty; taste the glaze before adding salty seasonings.

## Step 4: Carve and store

Carving instructions by type; leftovers 3–5 days refrigerated, 1–2 months frozen; reheat leftovers to 165°F; save the bone for soup.

## Guardrails

- Don't present a "fully cooked" ham reheat temperature for a raw or cook-before-eating ham.
- Leftover ham reheats to 165°F.
- Keep perishable ham out of the 40–140°F range for no more than 2 hours.
