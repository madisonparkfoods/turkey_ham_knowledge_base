# First-Timer FAQ

Short answers to the questions first-time holiday cooks ask most. Each links to the full detail.

## Planning and buying

**How big a turkey do I need?**
About 1–1½ lb per person, the higher end if you want leftovers. → [01](01-quick-reference.md), [03](03-buying-turkey-and-ham.md)

**Fresh or frozen?**
Either. Frozen is cheaper and flexible but needs days of fridge thawing. Fresh must be bought 1–2 days before. → [03](03-buying-turkey-and-ham.md)

**How many sides is enough?**
One starch, one stuffing, one or two vegetables, cranberry sauce, gravy, rolls, dessert. Add one per ~6 extra guests. → [02](02-planning-and-quantities.md)

**What can I make ahead?**
Cranberry sauce (a week), gravy (1–3 days), pies (1–2 days), mashed potatoes (2 days), stuffing and casseroles (assemble the day before), prepped vegetables (1–2 days). → [09](09-stuffing-and-sides.md), [10](10-day-of-timeline.md)

**What about vegetarian, gluten-free or allergic guests?**
Ask a week ahead; bake stuffing outside the bird; use cornstarch for gravy; label dishes; use separate serving spoons. → [02](02-planning-and-quantities.md)

**Turkey or ham?**
Ham is easier: most are fully cooked and only need reheating. Turkey is the tradition. Both is fine for bigger groups. → [02](02-planning-and-quantities.md), [07](07-ham.md)

## Thawing and prep

**When do I start thawing?**
About 1 day per 4–5 lb in the fridge. For Thanksgiving 2026 (Nov 26), move a 14–16 lb bird to the fridge by Saturday Nov 21. → [04](04-thawing.md)

**I forgot to thaw it.**
Cold water, ~30 min per lb, change the water every 30 min. Or cook from frozen (about 50% longer). → [04](04-thawing.md), [14](14-troubleshooting.md)

**What's the bag inside the turkey?**
The giblets (heart, gizzard, liver) and usually the neck. Remove them. Use the neck and giblets (not the liver) for gravy stock. → [04](04-thawing.md)

**Should I wash the turkey?**
No. It spreads bacteria around your kitchen and doesn't make it safer. Pat it dry. → [12](12-food-safety.md)

**Should I brine?**
Yes, a dry brine is the easiest way to a juicy, seasoned turkey, unless the turkey is kosher or "enhanced" (already salted). → [05](05-brining-and-seasoning.md)

**Do I need a roasting rack?**
It helps, but a bed of thick-sliced vegetables or coiled foil works. → [15](15-hosting-and-equipment.md)

## Cooking

**What temperature is it done?**
165°F in the breast (many cooks pull at ~160°F and let it rise during the rest), 170–175°F in the thigh. Ham: 140°F reheated, 145°F if raw. → [01](01-quick-reference.md)

**Where does the thermometer go?**
Thickest part of the breast, from the neck end, and the inner thigh; not touching bone. → [06](06-roasting-turkey.md)

**How long does it take?**
Roughly 13–15 minutes per pound at 325°F unstuffed, but go by temperature, not time. → [01](01-quick-reference.md)

**What time should the turkey go in?**
Work backward from dinner: rest (30–45 min) + roasting time + 45–60 min buffer. → [10](10-day-of-timeline.md)

**Should I baste?**
Not needed. It slows cooking and doesn't add moisture. → [06](06-roasting-turkey.md)

**The skin is too dark / not brown enough.**
Tent with foil / turn the heat up at the end. → [14](14-troubleshooting.md)

**Stuffing in the bird or separate?**
Separate is safer and easier. Inside the bird it must reach 165°F, which usually overcooks the breast. → [06](06-roasting-turkey.md), [09](09-stuffing-and-sides.md)

**How do I make gravy? It's lumpy / thin.**
Make it ahead from roasted wings, then add drippings on the day. Whisk or blend lumps; reduce or add slurry to thicken. → [08](08-gravy.md)

**How do I carve?**
Take off the legs, then remove each breast whole and slice it crosswise. → [11](11-carving-and-serving.md)

**How do I fit everything in one oven?**
Pies and casseroles the day before; sides go in while the turkey rests; slow cookers and the stovetop for the rest. → [10](10-day-of-timeline.md)

**The turkey is done early / running late.**
Early: tent and hold up to an hour. Late: raise heat, tent the breast, serve sides. → [10](10-day-of-timeline.md), [14](14-troubleshooting.md)

## Ham

**How do I know what kind of ham I have?**
The label: "fully cooked" / "ready to eat" means reheat to 140°F; "cook before eating" or fresh means cook to 145°F. → [07](07-ham.md)

**How do I keep a spiral ham from drying out?**
Cut side down, a little liquid in the pan, tightly covered, 325°F or lower, glaze only at the end. → [07](07-ham.md)

## Hosting

**Can I ask guests to bring dishes?**
Yes. Assign specific, oven-free dishes: pies, salads, rolls, drinks, ice. → [02](02-planning-and-quantities.md)

**What equipment do I need?**
Above all, an instant-read thermometer. Then a roasting pan, cutting board, sharp knife, foil, and a serving utensil for every dish. → [15](15-hosting-and-equipment.md)

## After the meal

**How fast must leftovers go in the fridge?**
Within 2 hours of serving. → [13](13-leftovers.md)

**How long do leftovers keep?**
Turkey 3–4 days in the fridge, 2–6 months frozen; ham 3–5 days. → [13](13-leftovers.md)

**What do I do with the carcass?**
Make stock within a day or two. → [13](13-leftovers.md)
