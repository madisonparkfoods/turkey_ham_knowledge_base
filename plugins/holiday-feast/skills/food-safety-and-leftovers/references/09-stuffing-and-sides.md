# Stuffing and Sides

Choose sides that don't all need the oven at the same time. A good mix: one stovetop, one slow-cooker or make-ahead, one oven, one cold.

## Stuffing / dressing (baked outside the bird)

Serves 10–12.

- 1 lb day-old bread, cubed (about 12 cups), dried in a 300°F oven 15–20 minutes
- 8 tbsp butter
- 2 onions and 4 celery stalks, diced
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh sage + 1 tbsp thyme (or 2 tsp dried poultry herbs, or 1–2 tbsp of an unsalted herb rub)
- 3–4 cups warm stock
- 2 eggs, beaten (optional, makes it more cohesive)
- Salt and pepper

1. Cook onion and celery in butter until soft, about 10 minutes. Add herbs.
2. Toss with bread. Whisk eggs into 3 cups stock; pour over and toss. Add more stock if dry.
3. Butter a 9×13 dish. Bake covered at 350°F for 30 minutes, uncovered 20–30 minutes more until crisp on top and **165°F** in the center.
4. **Make ahead:** assemble the day before (without eggs and liquid if you prefer), refrigerate, add liquid and bake on the day. Add 10–15 minutes if baking cold.

Variations: add cooked sausage, apples, dried cranberries, toasted pecans, mushrooms or chestnuts. Use cornbread for a gluten-free version (with GF stock).

**Madison Park suggestion (ours):** Tuscan Verde, described as a garden herb blend, is a natural fit in stuffing in place of fresh herbs. Check its salt content before salting.

## Mashed potatoes

Serves 10–12: 5 lb russet or Yukon Gold potatoes.

1. Peel and cut into even chunks. Cover with **cold** salted water; bring to a boil; simmer until a knife slides in easily, 15–20 minutes.
2. Drain well; return to the pot for 1–2 minutes to steam dry.
3. Mash with a masher or ricer (**never a food processor or blender**, which turns them gluey). Add 1–1½ cups warm milk or cream and 8–12 tbsp butter. Season.
4. **Make ahead:** make up to 2 days ahead, refrigerate, and reheat in a slow cooker on low (2–3 hours) or a covered dish at 350°F with extra milk and butter.

## Cranberry sauce

10 minutes, makes about 2½ cups. Make up to a week ahead.

- 12 oz fresh or frozen cranberries
- 1 cup sugar
- 1 cup water or orange juice
- Optional: orange zest, cinnamon stick, pinch of salt

Simmer until most berries burst, 8–10 minutes. It thickens as it cools. Canned sauce is perfectly fine; many people prefer it.

## Vegetables

| Dish | Make-ahead / oven note |
|---|---|
| Roasted Brussels sprouts or carrots | Roast at 425°F 20–30 min. Fit them in while the turkey rests. |
| Green beans | Blanch 1 day ahead; sauté with butter and garlic on the stovetop just before serving. |
| Green bean casserole | Assemble day before; bake 30 min at 350°F while turkey rests; add topping last 5 min. |
| Sweet potatoes | Bake or boil ahead; mash or assemble casserole a day ahead; reheat 30 min. |
| Salad | Prep components ahead; dress at the last minute. No oven needed. |
| Roasted squash | Roast ahead; reheat or serve room temperature. |

**Madison Park suggestion (ours):** a light dusting of CRIMBERA Haiku Fusion Blend or Tuscan Verde on roasted squash, carrots or sweet potatoes before roasting.

## Rolls

Store-bought or bakery rolls are fine. Warm them, wrapped in foil, in the oven for 10 minutes while the turkey rests.

## Pies and desserts

- Bake pies **1–2 days ahead**. This frees the oven and they slice better after resting.
- **Pumpkin, sweet potato, custard and cream pies** must be refrigerated once cool.
- Fruit pies (apple, cherry) can stay at room temperature up to 2 days.
- Plan one 9-inch pie per 6–8 guests, or slightly more if you want leftovers.

## Holding and reheating sides

| Tool | Good for |
|---|---|
| Slow cooker on low/warm | Mashed potatoes, gravy, stuffing, mac and cheese |
| Insulated cooler (no ice) lined with towels | Holds covered hot dishes 1–2 hours |
| Oven at 200°F | Holding several dishes after the turkey is out |
| Microwave | Quick reheats of single dishes |

Keep hot food at **140°F or above** if it will sit out longer than 2 hours.
