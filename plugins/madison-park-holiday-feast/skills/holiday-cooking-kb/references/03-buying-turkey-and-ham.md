# Buying a Turkey or Ham

## Turkey

### Fresh or frozen?

| | Fresh | Frozen |
|---|---|---|
| Buy | 1–2 days before cooking (order ahead for holidays) | Any time; keeps indefinitely frozen, best within a year |
| Pros | No thawing; often better texture | Cheaper, easy to find, flexible timing |
| Cons | Must be bought close to the day; can sell out | Thawing takes days of fridge space |

Neither is "better" for a first-timer. Frozen is fine if you start thawing on time.

### Reading the label

| Label | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Kosher** | Already salted during processing. **Do not brine.** |
| **Self-basting / enhanced / "contains up to X% of a solution"** | Injected with salt water or broth. **Do not brine.** Season with an unsalted rub. |
| **Natural** | Minimally processed, no artificial ingredients. Says nothing about how it was raised. |
| **Heritage** | Older breeds; more dark meat, less breast, cooks faster. Use a thermometer early. |
| **Pre-brined** | Already brined. Do not brine again. |
| **Free-range, organic, pasture-raised** | Raising standards; no change to cooking method. |
| **Halal** | Slaughtered according to Islamic practice. Not usually pre-salted, but check for "solution" on the label. |
| **Fully cooked / smoked / oven-roasted** (whole turkey, breast or parts) | Ready to eat. Reheat to 165°F; don't brine. Follow the package's reheating directions. |

### Size

- 1–1½ lb per person (see [01-quick-reference.md](01-quick-reference.md)).
- Above about 18 lb, consider two smaller birds or a bird plus a separate turkey breast.
- Make sure it fits: measure your roasting pan and oven. A 20 lb turkey needs a large roasting pan and about 9 inches of clearance.

### Turkey breast as an alternative

A bone-in turkey breast (5–8 lb) is the easiest option for small groups or as a backup alongside a whole bird. It cooks in about 1½–2½ hours at 325°F and has no dark meat to coordinate.

## Ham

### Ham types

| Type | Label words | Cooking needed |
|---|---|---|
| **City ham, fully cooked** (most common holiday ham) | "fully cooked," "ready to eat," "heat and serve" | Reheat to 140°F, or serve cold |
| **Spiral-sliced** | Fully cooked, pre-sliced around the bone | Reheat gently, covered, to 140°F; dries out easily |
| **Partially cooked / cook-before-eating** | "cook before eating," "cook thoroughly" | Cook to 145°F + 3 min rest |
| **Fresh ham** | Raw, uncured pork leg (no "cured" on label, pink raw meat) | Roast like pork to 145°F + 3 min rest |
| **Country ham** | Dry-cured, very salty, often uncooked | USDA suggests soaking 4–12 hours (many producers recommend longer), then simmering 20–25 min/lb before glazing. Follow the producer's instructions. |

### Bone-in or boneless?

- **Bone-in:** better flavor and texture, a bone for soup later, harder to carve. Plan ¾ lb per person.
- **Boneless:** easy to slice, more uniform, a bit more processed. Plan ⅓–½ lb per person.
- **Half hams:** "shank end" is easier to carve; "butt end" has more meat but an awkward bone.

### Buying tips

- Spiral hams are the easiest for first-time hosts: no carving, just reheat.
- Many hams come with a glaze packet. You can use it, or make your own (see [07-ham.md](07-ham.md)).
- Store an unopened, vacuum-packed fully cooked ham in the fridge and use by the package date.

## When to buy

| Item | When |
|---|---|
| Frozen turkey | 1–3 weeks ahead; start thawing on schedule |
| Fresh turkey | Order 1–2 weeks ahead; pick up 1–2 days before |
| Fully cooked ham | 1 week ahead (check use-by date) |
| Fresh ham | Order ahead from a butcher; pick up 1–3 days before |
| Spices and rubs (including Madison Park products) | 1–3 weeks ahead; specialty products can sell out or ship slowly near holidays |
