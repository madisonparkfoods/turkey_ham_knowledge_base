# Thawing a Frozen Turkey

Never thaw a turkey on the counter, in a garage, on a porch, or in hot water. The outside sits in the danger zone (40–140°F) while the middle is still frozen.

## Method 1: Refrigerator (recommended)

- Allow **about 24 hours for every 4–5 lb.**
- Keep the turkey in its wrapper, breast-up, on a rimmed tray or in a pan on the bottom shelf so juices can't drip on other food.
- Once thawed, it keeps **1–2 more days** in the fridge before cooking.

| Weight | Days in fridge |
|---|---|
| 4–12 lb | 1–3 days |
| 12–16 lb | 3–4 days |
| 16–20 lb | 4–5 days |
| 20–24 lb | 5–6 days |

These include about a day of cushion, which you'll also want for dry brining.

## Method 2: Cold water (when you're short on time)

- Allow **about 30 minutes per pound.**
- Keep the turkey in its original wrapper or a leak-proof bag. If water gets in, the meat gets waterlogged and can be contaminated.
- Submerge breast-down in cold tap water in a clean sink, cooler or large pot.
- **Change the water every 30 minutes** so it stays cold.
- **Cook immediately** after thawing.

| Weight | Hours |
|---|---|
| 4–12 lb | 2–6 |
| 12–16 lb | 6–8 |
| 16–20 lb | 8–10 |
| 20–24 lb | 10–12 |

## Method 3: Microwave

Only if the turkey fits and your microwave has a defrost setting by weight. Parts may start to cook, so **cook immediately** afterward. Rarely practical for a whole bird.

## Cooking a turkey that is still partly frozen

USDA says a frozen or partly frozen turkey can be cooked safely; it just takes about **50% longer** than a thawed one. You can't dry brine it, the giblet bag may be stuck inside (remove it with tongs once loosened), and the breast may overcook while the thighs catch up. Use a thermometer in several spots.

## How to tell it's thawed

- The breast and thighs feel soft when pressed, not hard.
- You can remove the neck and giblet bag from the cavities. If the cavity is still icy, run cold water through it.
- A few ice crystals deep inside are OK if you're cooking right away.

## Remove before cooking

- **Giblet bag** (usually in the neck cavity, under the skin flap).
- **Neck** (usually in the main cavity).
- Plastic or metal **leg clamp**: metal ones are oven-safe; plastic ones usually are too, but check the packaging. You can remove it if you want the thighs to cook a little faster.
- **Pop-up timer:** unreliable; ignore it and use a thermometer.

Save the neck and giblets (except the liver, which turns gravy bitter) for stock or gravy.
