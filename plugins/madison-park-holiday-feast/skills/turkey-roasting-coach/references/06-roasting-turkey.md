# Roasting a Turkey

## The one rule

**Dry turkey is overcooked turkey.** White meat is done at 165°F and dries out quickly past it; dark meat is better at 170–180°F. Every method below is a way to get both right at the same time. A good instant-read or leave-in probe thermometer matters more than any recipe.

## Where to put the thermometer

- **Breast:** thickest part, inserted from the neck end, horizontally into the center. Don't touch bone.
- **Thigh:** thickest part of the inner thigh, near but not touching the bone.
- **Stuffing (if in the bird):** center of the cavity. Must reach 165°F.
- Check more than one spot. Trust the lowest reading.

## Method A: Classic roast (the default)

For a 12–14 lb, dry-brined turkey. See [05-brining-and-seasoning.md](05-brining-and-seasoning.md) for the brine.

**Ingredients**

- 1 whole turkey, 12–14 lb, thawed, dry-brined 1–3 days
- 4 tbsp softened unsalted butter (or herb butter). Optional: mix in 2–3 tbsp of a salt-free rub such as Madison Park's Chairman of the Bird, so the butter adds flavor without adding to the dry brine's salt.
- 1 onion, quartered; 1 lemon, halved; 1 head garlic, halved; a small bunch of thyme, sage and rosemary
- 2 cups chicken or turkey stock

**Steps**

1. **Temper:** take the turkey out of the fridge about 1 hour before roasting. Heat oven to **425°F** with a rack in the lower third.
2. **Prep:** put aromatics loosely in the cavity. Rub butter over the skin (and some under the breast skin). Tuck wingtips behind the back. Tying legs is optional; loosely tied legs look neater, untied thighs cook a little faster.
3. **Pan:** set the turkey breast-up on a rack in a roasting pan. Pour stock into the pan to keep drippings from burning.
4. **High-heat start:** roast at 425°F for **30 minutes** to start browning.
5. **Lower the heat:** reduce to **325°F**. Roast until the breast reads about **160°F** and the thigh **170–175°F**. For 12–14 lb this is usually another **2–2½ hours**; start checking after about 1½ hours. Add water to the pan if it runs dry.
6. **Tent if needed:** if the breast browns too fast or runs ahead of the thighs, cover it loosely with foil.
7. **Rest:** transfer to a board, tent loosely, rest **30–45 minutes**. Carryover heat brings the breast to 165°F. Confirm with the thermometer. Make gravy meanwhile.
8. **Carve:** see [11-carving-and-serving.md](11-carving-and-serving.md).

**About basting:** opening the oven drops its temperature and lengthens cooking; basting doesn't make meat juicier. Skip it, or baste at most once or twice.

**Breast-side down?** Starting the bird breast-down for the first hour can protect the breast, but flipping a hot turkey is awkward and risky. Not recommended for first-timers.

## Method B: Spatchcock (butterflied) turkey

The fastest, most even method. The backbone is cut out and the bird is pressed flat, so the thighs get more heat and the breast less.

1. Turkey breast-side down. With heavy poultry shears, cut along both sides of the backbone and remove it (save for gravy stock).
2. Flip breast-up. Press firmly on the breastbone until it cracks and the bird lies flat. Tuck wingtips.
3. Dry brine as usual (it takes the salt even better flat).
4. Roast on a rack over a rimmed sheet pan at **450°F**. A 12–14 lb bird takes about **70–90 minutes**; start checking at 60.
5. Same temperatures: breast ~160°F, thigh 170–175°F, rest 20–30 minutes.

Trade-off: no whole-bird presentation at the table. Carve in the kitchen.

## Method C: Bone-in turkey breast

Great for small groups or as a backup alongside a whole bird.

- Dry brine 1–2 days (1 tbsp Diamond Crystal per 4–5 lb).
- Roast at 325°F (or 425°F for 20 minutes, then 325°F) until **160–165°F** in the thickest part, about **1½–2½ hours** for 5–7 lb.
- Rest 15–20 minutes.

## Method D: Stuffed turkey

Possible but harder. The stuffing in the center must reach **165°F**, and by the time it does the breast is usually overcooked. If you do it:

- Stuff **loosely** right before roasting (never the night before), about ¾ cup per pound of turkey.
- Use cooked or moist ingredients; raw-egg stuffings must reach 165°F.
- Add 30–45 minutes to cooking time; check the stuffing center separately.
- Spoon out all the stuffing right after roasting.

**Recommended instead:** bake stuffing in a separate dish ("dressing") and moisten it with pan drippings.

## Other methods (brief)

- **Smoked turkey:** smoke at 275–325°F. Lower temperatures make rubbery skin; finish hot or accept softer skin. Smaller birds (under 14 lb) are best; must reach 165°F. A smoky, slightly sweet rub (e.g., Madison Park's Stan's Big Red Bird Rub) suits this.
  - **Wood or pellets:** turkey takes smoke easily, so mild woods work best: apple, cherry, maple, pecan, or a little hickory. Pellet "turkey blends" are usually fruitwood-based. Mesquite over a long smoke can taste bitter.
  - Dry brine or inject (not both) before smoking; smoked skin doesn't crisp much, so dry the skin well.
- **Deep-fried turkey:** fast and delicious but the most dangerous holiday cooking. See the safety section in [12-food-safety.md](12-food-safety.md). Only fully thawed, completely dry birds; outdoors only. Inject for flavor rather than brining wet, and season the skin lightly after it's dry.
- **Oven bag (roasting bag):** speeds cooking and keeps meat moist; skin browns less. Buy the turkey size (fits up to about 24 lb). Follow the bag's instructions: usually shake 1 tbsp flour inside, cut a few small slits in the top, roast at 350°F in a pan at least 2 inches deep, and keep the bag clear of the oven walls and top element. Times are shorter than open roasting; check the temperature early.
- **Cheesecloth method:** soak a double layer of 100% cotton cheesecloth in melted butter (and a salt-free rub, if you like), drape it over the breast and legs, and roast as in Method A. Remove the cloth for the last 30–45 minutes so the skin browns. It keeps the breast from drying out and replaces basting. Keep the cloth tucked onto the pan, away from oven elements.
- **Grill (indirect heat):** treat like a 325–350°F oven with the turkey away from direct flame; rotate for even cooking.

## Roasting pans

- **Sturdy roasting pan with a rack:** best for a whole bird and for making gravy right in the pan on the stove.
- **Disposable foil roasting pan:** fine, but always set it on a rimmed sheet pan. A full foil pan bends when lifted and can spill hot fat. Don't put it on a burner for gravy; pour the drippings into a saucepan instead. Extra-large foil pans hold birds over 20 lb but check that they fit your oven first.
- **Spatchcock:** a rimmed sheet pan with a rack is all you need.
- No rack? Set the turkey on thick slices of onion, carrot and celery, or coils of foil.

## Turkey parts (legs, thighs, drumsticks, wings)

For dark-meat fans, small groups, or extra pieces alongside a whole bird:

- **Thighs and drumsticks:** roast at 375°F, about 1–1½ hours, until **175–180°F** near the bone. Dark meat is tender and forgiving at these temperatures. Braising (covered, partly submerged in stock, 325°F for about 2 hours) makes them fall-apart tender.
- **Wings:** roast at 375–400°F about 1 hour until browned and 175°F or more. Roasted wings and necks also make the best make-ahead gravy stock ([08-gravy.md](08-gravy.md)).
- **Boneless turkey breast roast:** roast at 325–350°F to 160–165°F; it cooks faster than bone-in, so start checking early. Season under any netting or on the surface.
- **Smoked turkey legs, wings or necks** from the store are usually fully cooked (check the label). Reheat them to 165°F to eat, or simmer them in greens, beans or soup for smoky flavor.

## Pan drippings

Don't discard them. Pour into a fat separator or measuring cup, let the fat rise, and use the juices plus some fat for gravy ([08-gravy.md](08-gravy.md)). If the turkey was wet-brined or dry-brined heavily, taste drippings before adding any salt to the gravy.
