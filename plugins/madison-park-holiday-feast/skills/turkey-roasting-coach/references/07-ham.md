# Holiday Ham

## Step 1: Identify the ham

Read the label; it decides everything.

| Label says | Type | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Fully cooked," "ready to eat," "heat and serve" | Cooked city ham | Reheat to **140°F** | Can also be served cold. Most holiday hams. |
| "Spiral sliced," fully cooked | Cooked, pre-sliced | Reheat to **140°F**, covered | Dries out fastest. Keep covered, use low heat. |
| "Cook before eating," "cook thoroughly" | Partially cooked cured ham | Cook to **145°F** + 3 min rest | Treat like raw pork. |
| No cure, raw pink pork leg | **Fresh ham** | Cook to **145°F** + 3 min rest | A pork roast, not "ham" flavor. Season and roast. |
| "Country ham," dry-cured | Country ham | Producer's instructions | USDA: soak 4–12 h (producers often say longer), simmer 20–25 min/lb, then glaze at 400°F. Very salty. |

Fully cooked hams **not** packaged in a USDA-inspected plant, and leftover ham, should be reheated to **165°F**.

## Step 2: Reheat a fully cooked ham (the common case)

**Goal:** heat it through without drying it out, then glaze.

1. Take the ham out of the fridge 1–2 hours before (no more than 2 hours). Heat oven to **325°F**.
2. Remove packaging and any plastic disc on the bone. Discard or set aside the glaze packet.
3. **Non-spiral hams:** score the fat in a ½-inch-deep diamond pattern. This helps the glaze cling.
4. Place the ham **cut side down** in a roasting pan. Add ½–1 cup water, stock, apple cider or juice to the pan.
5. **Cover tightly with foil.** (Especially important for spiral hams.)
6. Heat until the center reads about **120–130°F**:
   - Whole bone-in: 15–18 min/lb
   - Half bone-in: 18–24 min/lb
   - Spiral: 10–18 min/lb
   - Boneless: 10–15 min/lb
7. **Glaze:** uncover, raise oven to **400°F**, brush on glaze. Return for **15–25 minutes**, brushing once or twice more, until glossy and the center reaches **140°F**. Watch closely; sugar burns.
8. Rest 10–20 minutes before carving.

**Spiral ham tip:** keep it cut-side down and covered until glazing so the slices don't dry out. Some cooks reheat at 275–300°F for gentler heating; it just takes longer.

## Step 3: Cook a fresh (raw) ham

1. Score the skin/fat. Season generously with salt (dry brine 1–2 days at about 1 tbsp Diamond Crystal per 4–5 lb), pepper, garlic and herbs.
2. Roast at 325°F (some start at 450°F for 20 minutes to crisp the skin) until **145°F** in the thickest part, about 22–26 min/lb for a whole leg.
3. Rest at least 3 minutes (15–20 for a large roast) before carving.

## Glazes

Apply only in the last 15–25 minutes. Basic formula: **sweet + tangy + spice.**

| Glaze | Combine |
|---|---|
| Brown sugar mustard | ½ cup brown sugar, 2 tbsp Dijon, 2 tbsp cider vinegar, pinch of cloves |
| Honey orange | ⅓ cup honey, zest and juice of 1 orange, 1 tbsp Dijon, ½ tsp ground ginger |
| Maple bourbon | ⅓ cup maple syrup, 2 tbsp bourbon, 1 tbsp whole-grain mustard |
| Pineapple | ½ cup pineapple juice, ½ cup brown sugar, 1 tbsp Dijon |
| Cranberry | ½ cup cranberry sauce, 2 tbsp honey, 1 tbsp Dijon |

Simmer the glaze 3–5 minutes to thicken it so it clings.

**Madison Park serving suggestions:** 1–2 tsp of Colonial Tavern (described by Madison Park as slightly sweet, spicy and savory with a touch of salt and heat) stirred into the brown sugar mustard glaze; or CRIMBERA Haiku Fusion Blend (Urfa biber, sumac, smoked paprika, hibiscus, cassia) in the cranberry glaze for a warm, tart edge. Taste first: hams are already salty.

## Carving a ham

- **Spiral:** cut along the bone to release the slices; they fall away.
- **Bone-in shank or whole:** set it on its flattest side. Cut a few slices parallel to the bone from the thinner side to make a stable base, turn it onto that base, then slice down to the bone and cut along the bone to release the slices.
- **Boneless:** slice across into ¼-inch slices.

## Leftovers

Fully cooked ham keeps **3–5 days** refrigerated, **1–2 months** frozen for best quality. Save the bone for soup (split pea, bean, collards). See [13-leftovers.md](13-leftovers.md).
