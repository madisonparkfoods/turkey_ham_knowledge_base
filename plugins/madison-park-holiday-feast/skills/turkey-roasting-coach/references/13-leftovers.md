# Leftovers

## Cool quickly

- Get leftovers into the fridge **within 2 hours** of serving (1 hour if the room is above 90°F).
- Carve all the meat off the bones first. Whole carcasses cool too slowly.
- Use **shallow containers** (2 inches deep or less) so food cools fast. Hot food can go straight into the fridge; you don't need to let it cool on the counter.
- Don't overload the fridge; air needs to circulate.

## How long things keep

| Food | Refrigerator (≤40°F) | Freezer (0°F, best quality) |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked turkey | 3–4 days | 2–6 months |
| Stuffing | 3–4 days | 1 month |
| Gravy | 1–2 days | 2–3 months |
| Mashed potatoes | 3–5 days | 1 month (texture changes) |
| Cooked vegetables, casseroles | 3–4 days | 2–3 months |
| Cranberry sauce (homemade) | 10–14 days | 2 months |
| Fully cooked ham (sliced or whole, opened) | 3–5 days | 1–2 months |
| Pumpkin / custard pie | 3–4 days | 1–2 months |
| Fruit pie | 2 days room temp, 4–5 days fridge | 4 months |
| Turkey or ham stock | 3–4 days | 4–6 months |

Frozen food stays safe indefinitely at 0°F; the times above are for quality.

## Reheating

- Reheat leftovers to **165°F**.
- Gravy: bring to a rolling boil.
- Turkey slices stay moist when reheated covered with a splash of stock or gravy at 325°F, or in a covered skillet with a little stock.
- Microwave: cover, stir or rotate, and let stand 1–2 minutes so heat evens out.

## Freezing

- Wrap tightly or use freezer bags with the air pressed out.
- Freeze turkey in meal-sized portions, ideally with a little gravy or stock to prevent drying.
- Label with contents and date.
- Thaw in the fridge overnight, or reheat straight from frozen.

## Turkey stock from the carcass

1. Break up the carcass; put it in a large pot with any bones, the neck, onion, carrot, celery, a bay leaf and peppercorns.
2. Cover with cold water. Bring to a simmer (not a boil) and cook 3–4 hours, skimming occasionally.
3. Strain, cool quickly (divide into smaller containers), refrigerate. Skim the fat once cold.
4. Use within 3–4 days or freeze.

Make it within a day or two of the holiday, keeping the carcass refrigerated.

## Leftover ideas

- Turkey sandwiches with cranberry sauce and stuffing
- Turkey soup or turkey and rice soup from the stock
- Turkey pot pie or shepherd's pie with leftover mashed potatoes
- Turkey enchiladas, tacos or chili
- Ham and bean soup or split pea soup with the ham bone
- Ham and cheese strata or quiche
- Fried rice with diced ham or turkey
- Stuffing waffles; mashed potato pancakes

**Madison Park suggestion:** a spoonful of Tuscan Verde or Chairman of the Bird seasons turkey soup or pot pie in place of fresh herbs. Taste before salting.

## Sending leftovers home with guests

Pack in shallow containers, tell guests to refrigerate within 2 hours, and have them use it within 3–4 days. Have containers or foil ready before dinner.
