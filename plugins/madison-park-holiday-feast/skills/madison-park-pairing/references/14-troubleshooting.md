# Troubleshooting

Find the problem; do the first fix that applies.

## Before cooking

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Forgot to thaw the turkey** | Cold-water thaw: ~30 min per lb, water changed every 30 min ([04-thawing.md](04-thawing.md)). Or cook from frozen: about 50% longer, remove giblets with tongs when loosened. Or spatchcock as soon as it's thawed enough to cut, which cooks fastest. |
| **Turkey still icy inside on the day** | Run cold water through the cavity until it loosens. Remove giblets. Cook; expect it to take longer. |
| **Turkey won't fit the pan or oven** | Spatchcock it, or separate into breast and legs and roast them on a sheet pan (the legs need a little longer). |
| **No roasting rack** | Make a bed of thick-cut onions, carrots and celery, or loosely coiled foil "snakes," and set the turkey on top. |
| **No roasting pan** | Use a large rimmed sheet pan (spatchcock or parts) or a disposable foil pan set on a sheet pan for support. |
| **Bought a kosher or enhanced turkey and already brined** | Rinse it briefly (then clean the sink well), pat dry, skip adding salt to butter, rub and gravy. Taste drippings before seasoning. |
| **No thermometer** | Buy one if at all possible; it is the single most important tool. Without one: juices run clear when the thigh is pierced, the leg wiggles loosely, and the meat at the thigh bone isn't pink. These are much less reliable. |

## During roasting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Skin browning too fast** | Tent the breast or the whole bird loosely with foil. |
| **Skin not browning** | For the last 20–30 minutes, raise the oven to 425–450°F. Make sure the skin isn't wet and the bird isn't tented. |
| **Breast done, thighs not** | Remove the turkey, carve off the breast, cover it, and return the legs to the oven. Or tent the breast with foil and continue. |
| **Drippings burning** | Add 1–2 cups of water or stock to the pan. |
| **Running much later than expected** | Check the oven temperature with an oven thermometer. Raise to 350–375°F, tent the breast. Serve sides and appetizers. Spatchcocking a half-cooked bird is possible but awkward; carving it into parts and finishing them on a sheet pan is faster. |
| **Done way early** | Tent loosely, cover with towels, hold up to 1 hour. Slice right before serving and moisten with warm stock. |
| **Smoke from the oven** | Usually fat or drippings burning on the oven floor or pan. Add liquid to the pan; wipe spills when safe. |
| **Power or oven failure** | Spatchcock or cut into parts and use a grill (indirect heat), a neighbor's oven, or a large slow cooker for a breast. |

## After roasting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Turkey is pink near the bone but reads 165°F+** | It's safe. Young birds and smoked birds often stay pink near bones. Trust the thermometer. |
| **Turkey is under 165°F after carving** | Put the pieces on a sheet pan, cover with foil, and finish in a 350°F oven, or simmer slices briefly in hot stock until they reach 165°F. |
| **Dry turkey** | Slice, arrange on a warm platter, ladle hot stock or gravy over it, and cover for a few minutes. Serve with plenty of gravy. |
| **Too salty** | Serve with unsalted gravy and sides; slice thinly. Next time use less salt or don't combine brines. |
| **Bland** | Serve with well-seasoned gravy; sprinkle sliced meat with flaky salt and a little rub or fresh herbs. |
| **Gravy problems** | See [08-gravy.md](08-gravy.md#fixing-gravy). |

## Sides

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Gluey mashed potatoes** | Can't fully fix. Turn them into a baked potato casserole with cheese and breadcrumbs, or potato cakes. Next time use a masher/ricer, not a blender. |
| **Dry stuffing** | Drizzle with warm stock, cover, and bake 10 more minutes. |
| **Soggy stuffing** | Bake uncovered at 400°F for 10–15 minutes to crisp the top. |
| **Pie crust browning too fast** | Shield the edges with foil strips or a pie shield. |
| **Cracked pumpkin pie** | Cover with whipped cream. It's slightly overbaked but tastes fine. |
| **Burned a dish** | Don't stir the burned part in. Scoop off the unburned top into a new dish. If it's a nonessential side, drop it; nobody will miss one dish. |

## Ham

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Spiral ham drying out** | Re-cover tightly with foil, add liquid to the pan, lower the oven to 275°F. Serve with extra glaze or pan juices. |
| **Glaze burning** | Lower to 350°F and tent; apply glaze only in the last 15–25 minutes. |
| **Ham too salty** | Serve with sweet sides, a fruit chutney or mustard sauce; slice thin. For country ham, soak longer next time. |

## People

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| **More guests than planned** | Add a quick starch (rice, pasta, extra rolls), slice the meat thinner, put out extra bread and cheese. |
| **Guest arrives with an unannounced dietary need** | Point out the naturally safe dishes. Plain roasted vegetables, salad and cranberry sauce cover most needs. |
| **Everyone's in the kitchen** | Give them jobs: drinks, setting out serving dishes, filling water glasses. |
