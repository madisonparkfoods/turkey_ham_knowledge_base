# Food Safety

Holiday meals combine large pieces of poultry, crowded fridges, long buffets and many hands. These are the points that matter most.

## The four basics (USDA)

1. **Clean:** wash hands for 20 seconds with soap after touching raw turkey, ham or eggs. Clean counters, sinks and tools with hot soapy water.
2. **Separate:** keep raw poultry and its juices away from ready-to-eat food. Use a separate cutting board for raw meat. Store raw turkey on the bottom shelf.
3. **Cook:** use a food thermometer. Poultry and stuffing to **165°F**; see [01-quick-reference.md](01-quick-reference.md) for ham.
4. **Chill:** refrigerate leftovers within **2 hours** (1 hour above 90°F). Fridge at **40°F or below**; freezer at **0°F**.

## Don't wash the turkey

Rinsing raw poultry sprays bacteria around the sink and counters up to a couple of feet away and does nothing to make the meat safer; cooking does that. Pat it dry with paper towels instead and throw them away. If you rinsed it anyway, clean and sanitize the sink and everything nearby.

## Thawing

Only in the fridge, in cold water (changed every 30 minutes), or in the microwave, with immediate cooking for the last two. Never on the counter. See [04-thawing.md](04-thawing.md).

## Brining

Keep a wet brine **below 40°F** for the whole time. In a cooler, use plenty of ice and check with a thermometer. Discard used brine.

## Stuffing

- Stuffing cooked in the bird must reach **165°F at its center**.
- Stuff right before roasting; never stuff the night before.
- Never partially cook a stuffed turkey to finish later.
- Safest: bake it separately.

## The thermometer

- Calibrate an instant-read thermometer: in a glass of ice water it should read **32°F**. Adjust or note the offset.
- Put in fresh batteries before the holiday.
- Pop-up timers are not reliable.

## Cross-contamination traps on the day

- The **platter that held raw turkey** must be washed before it holds cooked turkey.
- **Tongs and brushes** used on raw meat need washing before touching cooked food.
- **Brine bags and wrappers** go straight in the trash.
- **Glaze or marinade** that touched raw meat must be boiled before serving as a sauce.

## Buffet safety

- Hot food: 140°F or above. Cold food: 40°F or below.
- Two-hour limit for anything in between. Put out smaller portions and refill from the kitchen.
- Pumpkin and custard pies, whipped cream, and dairy-based dips need the fridge.

## Deep-frying a turkey

Turkey fryers cause fires, burns and property damage every year. If you fry:

- **Outdoors only**, on a flat, non-combustible surface, at least 10 feet from the house, deck, garage, trees and anything flammable. Never in a garage or under an overhang.
- **Fully thawed and completely dry** turkey. Ice or water in oil causes violent boiling over.
- **Measure the oil level first**: put the turkey in the empty pot, cover with water by an inch or two, remove the turkey, and mark the water line. Fill oil only to that line. Dry the pot completely.
- **Turn the burner off** before lowering or raising the turkey, then relight.
- Lower the bird **slowly** with the hook or basket; wear long sleeves, oven mitts and eye protection.
- Never leave the fryer unattended. Keep children and pets away. Keep a **Class B (or ABC) fire extinguisher** nearby. **Never use water on an oil fire.**
- Let the oil cool completely (hours) before moving it.
- Turkeys under about 14 lb are safest to fry.
- **Fryer oil:** use an oil with a high smoke point, such as peanut, canola or another oil sold for frying. Heat to the fryer maker's temperature (usually about 350°F) and keep a frying thermometer in the oil. If you use peanut oil, tell guests in case of a peanut allergy. Strained, cooled oil can be stored sealed and reused a few times; never pour used oil down the drain.

Consider an oil-less fryer or roasting instead if any of these steps can't be done.

## Pets

Cooked turkey bones splinter and are dangerous for dogs and cats. Keep the carcass, string, foil and fatty scraps out of reach and in a closed trash bin.

## When in doubt, throw it out

If food sat out more than 2 hours, smells off, or you're unsure whether it reached a safe temperature, discard it.
