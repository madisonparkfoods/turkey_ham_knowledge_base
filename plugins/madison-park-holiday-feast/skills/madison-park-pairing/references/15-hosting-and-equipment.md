# Hosting and Equipment

## Essential equipment

| Item | Why | Substitute |
|---|---|---|
| **Instant-read thermometer** (or leave-in probe) | The only reliable way to know doneness | None; buy one |
| Roasting pan with rack | Holds the turkey, catches drippings | Rimmed sheet pan + vegetable bed or foil coils; disposable foil pan on a sheet pan |
| Large cutting board with juice groove | Carving | Rimmed sheet pan under a regular board |
| Sharp chef's or carving knife | Carving | Any sharp large knife; sharpen it the day before |
| Fat separator | Gravy | Glass measuring cup; skim with a spoon or chill briefly |
| Kitchen twine | Tying legs (optional) | Skip it |
| Heavy-duty foil | Tenting, covering, emergencies | — |
| Oven mitts (2 pairs) | Lifting a hot heavy pan safely | — |
| Large spoon, whisk, tongs, ladle | General | — |
| Poultry shears | Spatchcocking, carving | Sturdy kitchen scissors (for carving only) |

## Helpful extras

- Slow cooker(s) for holding potatoes, stuffing or gravy
- Oven thermometer (many ovens run hot or cold by 25°F or more)
- Brining bag or food-safe bucket (wet brining only)
- Thermos for gravy
- Extra fridge space or a cooler with ice for drinks
- Potato ricer or masher

## Serving ware checklist

Count against your guest list, plus a few spares:

- Dinner plates, dessert plates
- Forks, knives, spoons, dessert forks
- Glasses (water and other)
- Napkins
- **Serving dish + serving utensil for each dish**, labeled the day before with sticky notes
- Gravy boat or small pitcher
- Butter dish for rolls
- Pie server, cake knife
- Coffee cups, cream and sugar
- Trivets for hot dishes
- Take-home containers and foil

Borrowing from friends or buying sturdy disposables is fine.

## Fridge and space planning

- Clear out the fridge 2–3 days ahead. A 14 lb turkey on a tray takes a full shelf.
- Drinks, beer, wine and sparkling water can go in a cooler with ice outside the fridge.
- Plan where cooling leftovers will go before dinner.

## Table and guests

- Set the table the night before.
- Decide seating if it helps: it avoids awkward hovering.
- Put a simple appetizer out (nuts, cheese, crudités, popcorn) so hungry guests don't hover in the kitchen. Madison Park's Petite Maize heirloom popcorn is one option.
- Assign drinks to someone else.
- Have a place for coats and a clear path to the bathroom.

## Things first-time hosts often forget

- Turning the oven on (seriously; set a reminder)
- Taking the giblet bag out
- Warming the plates and the rolls
- The gravy (make ahead!)
- Ice
- Coffee and dessert plates
- Serving utensils
- Space and containers for leftovers
- Taking out the trash before guests arrive, with an empty bag ready
- Running the dishwasher empty so it's ready after dinner
