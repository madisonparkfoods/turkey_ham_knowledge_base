# Quick Reference

## Safe internal temperatures (USDA FSIS)

| Food | Minimum internal temperature |
|---|---|
| Turkey and all poultry (breast, thigh, wings, ground) | 165°F (74°C) |
| Stuffing (in or out of the bird) | 165°F (74°C) |
| Fresh (raw) ham, or cured ham labeled "cook before eating" | 145°F (63°C) + 3-minute rest |
| Fully cooked ham from a USDA-inspected plant, reheating | 140°F (60°C) |
| Fully cooked ham NOT from a USDA-inspected plant, or leftover ham | 165°F (74°C) |
| Leftovers and casseroles, reheating | 165°F (74°C) |
| Egg dishes, ground meat | 160°F (71°C) |

**Quality targets for turkey:** many cooks pull the bird when the breast reads about 160°F and the thigh 170–175°F, then let carryover heat during resting bring the breast to 165°F. Confirm it reaches 165°F before carving. If you are not comfortable with that, pull at 165°F; the breast will be slightly firmer but still good if dry-brined.

**Danger zone:** 40–140°F (4–60°C). Perishable food should not sit in it more than 2 hours total (1 hour if the room is above 90°F).

## Thawing a frozen turkey

| Method | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerator (≤40°F) | ~24 hours per 4–5 lb | Safest. Thawed bird keeps 1–2 more days in the fridge. |
| Cold water | ~30 minutes per lb | Bird in leak-proof bag, fully submerged; change water every 30 minutes; cook immediately. |
| Microwave | Per manufacturer | Only if it fits; cook immediately. |

| Turkey weight | Fridge thaw | Cold-water thaw |
|---|---|---|
| 4–12 lb | 1–3 days | 2–6 hours |
| 12–16 lb | 3–4 days | 6–8 hours |
| 16–20 lb | 4–5 days | 8–10 hours |
| 20–24 lb | 5–6 days | 10–12 hours |

**Thanksgiving 2026 is Thursday, November 26.** For a 14 lb bird in the fridge, start thawing by Saturday or Sunday, November 21–22.

## Roasting time estimates, whole turkey at 325°F (USDA)

| Weight | Unstuffed | Stuffed |
|---|---|---|
| 8–12 lb | 2¾–3 h | 3–3½ h |
| 12–14 lb | 3–3¾ h | 3½–4 h |
| 14–18 lb | 3¾–4¼ h | 4–4¼ h |
| 18–20 lb | 4¼–4½ h | 4¼–4¾ h |
| 20–24 lb | 4½–5 h | 4¾–5¼ h |

Start checking temperature 30–45 minutes before the low end. A 425°F start (see 06) shortens these slightly. A spatchcocked 12–14 lb bird at 450°F takes about 70–90 minutes.

## Ham reheating estimates at 325°F

| Ham | Approx. time |
|---|---|
| Fully cooked, bone-in whole | 15–18 min/lb |
| Fully cooked, bone-in half | 18–24 min/lb |
| Fully cooked, spiral-sliced | 10–18 min/lb (covered) |
| Fully cooked, boneless | 10–15 min/lb |
| Fresh (raw) ham, whole leg | 22–26 min/lb to 145°F |

## Brining ratios

| Method | Ratio | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Dry brine | 1 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt per 4–5 lb (use about half as much Morton kosher, which is denser) | 1–3 days, uncovered for the last day |
| Wet brine | ~1 cup Diamond Crystal kosher salt per gallon water (≈ ¾ cup Morton kosher, ≈ ½ cup table salt) | 12–24 hours, kept below 40°F |

**Never brine a turkey labeled kosher, self-basting, "enhanced," or "contains up to X% solution." It is already salted.**
