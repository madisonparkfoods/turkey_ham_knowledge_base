# The Countdown and Day-Of Timeline

## How to build any timeline: work backward

1. Pick the **serving time**.
2. Subtract the **turkey's rest** (30–45 min). That's when the turkey comes out.
3. Subtract the **estimated roasting time** plus a **45–60 minute buffer**. That's when it goes in.
4. Subtract **1 hour** for tempering. That's when it leaves the fridge.
5. Fit everything else into the rest window and the time before the turkey goes in.

The buffer matters: turkeys are often done earlier or later than estimated. An early turkey can rest up to about an hour under a loose tent and foil-covered towels and still be hot. A late turkey can't be rushed without risking the breast.

## Sample day: 12–14 lb turkey, dinner at 4:00 PM, one oven

| Time | Task |
|---|---|
| 9:00 AM | Coffee. Read through the plan. Clear counters and the dishwasher. |
| 10:30 AM | Turkey out of fridge to temper. Prep aromatics and butter. |
| 11:15 AM | Oven to 425°F. |
| 11:30 AM | **Turkey in.** Set a timer for 30 minutes. |
| 12:00 PM | Lower oven to 325°F. |
| 12:00–1:30 PM | Stovetop and prep work: peel potatoes, set out serving dishes and utensils (label them with sticky notes), get the bar/drinks ready. |
| 1:30 PM | First temperature check. |
| 2:00 PM | Potatoes on to boil (or make-ahead potatoes into the slow cooker). |
| ~2:30 PM | Check every 15–20 minutes. When breast ~160°F and thigh 170–175°F: **turkey out**, tent loosely to rest. |
| 2:45 PM | Oven up to 350–425°F for sides: stuffing, casseroles, roasted vegetables. Reheat make-ahead gravy and add drippings. Mash potatoes. |
| 3:15 PM | Rolls into the oven, wrapped in foil. Cook green beans on the stovetop. |
| 3:30 PM | Confirm turkey breast reached 165°F. **Carve.** |
| 3:45 PM | Everything to platters and the table. Gravy into a warmed gravy boat or thermos. |
| 4:00 PM | **Eat.** |
| By 6:00 PM | Leftovers in the fridge (2-hour rule). |

**Spatchcock version:** turkey in at 1:45 PM at 450°F, out about 3:00–3:15 PM, carve at 3:30 PM. This frees the oven for most of the day.

**Ham version (fully cooked, 8–10 lb spiral):** ham in at 1:00 PM covered at 325°F; uncover and glaze at 3:15 PM at 400°F; out at 3:35 PM; serve at 4:00 PM.

## Juggling one oven

- Bake **pies and casseroles the day before** where possible.
- Use the **turkey's rest** for anything that needs high heat (roasted vegetables, stuffing, rolls).
- Move potatoes, gravy and stuffing to **slow cookers**.
- Use the **stovetop** for green beans, gravy, cranberry sauce.
- Assign **cold dishes** (salads, relish trays) to guests.
- A countertop toaster oven or air fryer can handle rolls or a small vegetable dish.
- Don't stack too many dishes on one rack: air can't circulate and everything cooks slower.

## Two mains (turkey and ham)

The ham needs only to reheat at 325°F, the same temperature as the turkey's main phase. Put it on a lower rack, covered, about 2–2½ hours before serving, and glaze it during the turkey's rest when the oven goes up for sides. If the oven is too full, reheat a spiral ham in a slow cooker (6–8 lb fits a 6–7 quart cooker; 3–4 hours on low with ½ cup liquid, glaze brushed on at the end).

## If things go off schedule

- **Turkey done early:** tent loosely with foil, cover with clean towels, hold up to 1 hour. Carve close to serving and pour a little warm stock over the sliced meat.
- **Turkey running late:** raise the oven to 350–375°F, tent the breast with foil, and serve the sides first as a "first course." Don't cut into a turkey that isn't at temperature.
