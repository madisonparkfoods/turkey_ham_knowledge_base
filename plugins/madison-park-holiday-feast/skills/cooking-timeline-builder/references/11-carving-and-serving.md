# Carving and Serving

## Rest first

Resting lets juices settle and lets carryover heat finish the breast. Tent the turkey loosely with foil (tight foil steams the skin soft).

- Whole turkey: **30–45 minutes**
- Turkey breast: 15–20 minutes
- Ham: 10–20 minutes

It will still be hot enough to serve after 45 minutes.

## Tools

- A sharp chef's knife (8–10 inches) or a carving knife
- A large cutting board with a juice groove, set on a damp towel so it doesn't slide
- Tongs or a carving fork
- A warm platter

## Carving a turkey (kitchen method)

Carving in the kitchen and bringing a platter to the table is easier and neater than carving at the table.

1. **Remove the legs:** pull a leg away from the body and slice through the skin between leg and breast. Press the leg outward until the hip joint pops, then cut through the joint. Repeat on the other side.
2. **Separate drumstick and thigh:** cut through the joint between them (find it by wiggling the drumstick).
3. **Thigh meat:** cut along the thigh bone to remove the meat in one piece; slice it.
4. **Remove the breasts whole:** cut down along one side of the breastbone, keeping the knife against the ribs, and peel the whole breast half off. Repeat on the other side.
5. **Slice the breasts crosswise**, against the grain, into ½-inch slices, keeping skin on top. This gives every slice a bit of skin and is much juicier than slicing from the whole bird.
6. **Wings:** pull outward and cut through the shoulder joint.
7. **Arrange:** sliced breast in the center, dark meat and drumsticks around it, wings at the ends. Garnish with herbs or citrus.
8. Pour a spoonful of warm stock or drippings over the meat to keep it moist.

Save the carcass for stock ([13-leftovers.md](13-leftovers.md)).

## Serving setup

- **Buffet** works better than passing dishes for 10+ people. Order: plates → turkey/ham → stuffing → potatoes → gravy → vegetables → cranberry → rolls → napkins/cutlery at the end.
- Put **gravy at the end of the mains** so people pour it on what they've already taken.
- Label dishes, especially those that are vegetarian, gluten-free, or contain nuts.
- Use a **separate serving spoon for every dish** to avoid allergen cross-contact.
- Warm plates in a 150–200°F oven (or the dishwasher's dry cycle) so food stays hot longer.
- Put the 2-hour clock in your head: start putting leftovers away about 1½ hours after serving.
